Yesterday, my mom was saying during lunch that my niece always destroyed her toys because she wanted to know what was inside them.

This is the soul of a future engineer. I used to do the same thing as a kid 😂

Why is this interesting?

Because the impulse to create things is one of the most important characteristics someone can develop during life. Most kids naturally have this instinct. They want to understand how the world works. They take things apart. They test ideas. They create imaginary worlds. They build things that make no sense.

Then, somehow, many people lose this. We grow up hearing that creating is risky. Starting something is risky. Building a company is risky. Writing online is risky.
Launching a product is risky. Showing your ideas to the world is risky.

So people slowly become passive consumers instead of creators. They only watch. Only scroll. Only complain. Only wait for permission.

But the people who change their own lives usually have one thing in common.

They build.

- Sometimes they build companies.
- Sometimes software.
- Sometimes communities.
- Sometimes books.
- Sometimes a better version of themselves.

The creation itself is not the important part. The important part is developing the courage to transform an idea into something real. I genuinely believe that creating things changes the way you see the world. Once you build something, you stop seeing products, companies, apps, books, or businesses as magic. You start realizing that everything around you was created by ordinary people that simply decided to start.

This mindset is powerful.

Because the moment you understand that the world is buildable, your life also becomes buildable. You no longer feel trapped waiting for someone to save you.

You start asking yourself:

"What can I create to improve this situation?"

And honestly, this mentality compounds over time. Your first idea will probably be bad. Your first project will probably fail. Your first attempt may even feel embarrassing.

But every creation changes you a little.

The goal is not becoming famous. Not becoming rich overnight. Not building the next billion dollar startup. The goal is keeping alive that childhood instinct that says:

"I want to know how this works."

And then having the courage to build your own version of it.
